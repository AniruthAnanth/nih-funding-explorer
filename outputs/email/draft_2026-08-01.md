Subject: RE: MGB Department of Surgery — how we rank on NIH funding

Hi Dr. Yang,

One thing up front on what is answerable. There is no systematic source for
department-level **revenue** — individual departments publish their own figures,
as ours does ($74M of research expenditures in 2024, $35M of it NIH), but no one
collects that consistently across institutions, so a revenue ranking cannot be
built. What is public and complete is **NIH funding**, so I built the analysis on
NIH's ExPORTER files for FY2021–FY2025. Worth keeping in view that our own slide
puts NIH at 47% of the department's research spending: any NIH ranking, including
this one, is measuring about half the picture and none of the DOD, foundation,
industry or philanthropic support.

The complication is that NIH assigns a department code only to universities, not
to independent hospitals. MGB is therefore **absent from every published NIH
departmental ranking**, including the Blue Ridge Institute rankings that are the
usual reference — their FY2025 surgery table lists 75 institutions and not one
is a hospital. To fix that I rebuilt each investigator's department from their
dated PubMed affiliations, applied it to all 27 uncoded recipients rather than
only to MGB, and checked it three ways. Against NIH's own department field where
both exist, Cohen's κ 0.92. Against Blue Ridge institution by institution for
FY2025 surgery, 55 of 74 agree **to the dollar**. And against our own published
number — the $35M on the departmental slide — the reconstruction gives $30.8M for
FY2024, or 88%, which is the right side of correct for a figure I am calling a
lower bound.

Taking your three questions in order, FY2021–FY2025:

- **Total funding — MGB is 1st**, at **$266.0M**, ahead of Duke ($220.8M), WashU
  ($154.1M) and Michigan ($131.7M).

- **Number of grants — MGB is 1st**, with **134 distinct grants**, ahead of
  Michigan (106) and Duke (97).

- **Number of R01s — MGB is 2nd**, with **56 distinct R01 grants** to Michigan's
  59. Worth being precise here, because the two ways of counting disagree: if you
  count *award-years* instead — one grant counted once per year it was funded —
  MGB leads with 179 to Michigan's 165, because our R01s run longer. Both are on
  the site. **M awards no longer exist anywhere**: M01 general clinical research
  centres were retired into the CTSA programme, whose successors are U-series, so
  "R01 and M awards" is really just R01s.

One caveat to have ready: these MGB figures are MGH and BWH combined, and they
remain two departments with two chairs. Ranked separately over five years,
**MGH is 2nd at $160.7M and BWH 5th at $105.3M**. Both readings are on the site.

Two peers also appear that no published ranking contains at any position —
**Memorial Sloan Kettering 7th at $91.3M and Boston Children's 15th at $75.6M**.
Their absence everywhere else is the same coding artifact, not a result.

**Across specialties**, MGB's Department of Medicine is **$1.66B** over the same
period, larger than any other department of medicine in the country (NYU
Grossman $1.27B, UCSF $1.16B). Nationally surgery is the 19th largest department
of the 44 NIH names, and departments of medicine run roughly ten times larger
than departments of surgery — so a surgery-to-medicine comparison measures a
structural difference in how NIH funds research, not a difference in performance.

On the international question, NIH data cannot answer it. Non-US organizations
received **0.69%** of all NIH funding over the period, and China has five NIH
recipients totalling $5.9M — a Shanghai department could be the largest in the
world and would be invisible here. I am still looking for a source that supports
a real international comparison and will follow up separately.
